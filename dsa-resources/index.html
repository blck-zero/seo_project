Free DSA Resources for Beginners — Checked & Curated

This page collects every free resource we recommend across the DSA Roadmap into one place. For each topic, we list one primary resource (the one we think is best for an Indian B.Tech beginner) and one alternative. We explain why we picked each one.

How we pick resources: We check that each resource is (1) completely free, (2) beginner-friendly, (3) covers the topic well enough to solve the starter problems on our Practice Problems page, and (4) actively maintained as of our last check. We have no affiliate deals with any of these sites.

Complexity Basics

Roadmap position: Prerequisite (Step 0)

  • Primary: Khan Academy — Asymptotic Notation — visual and interactive, starts from zero. Best for students who dislike reading long text.
  • Alternative: CS Dojo — Introduction to Big O Notation (YouTube) — 15 minutes, clear examples, good if you prefer video.

Arrays

Roadmap position: Step 1

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Array Data Structure Guide — comprehensive written guide covering operations, patterns, and complexity. Good reference to come back to.
  • Alternative: Kunal Kushwaha — Complete Arrays Tutorial (YouTube) — long-form video with Java, popular in the Indian student community.

Strings

Roadmap position: Step 2

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — String Data Structure — covers immutability, common operations, and problem patterns.
  • Alternative: take U forward — Strings Playlist (YouTube) — Hindi explanations, very popular among Indian B.Tech students.

Searching & Sorting

Roadmap position: Step 3

  • Primary: Khan Academy — Algorithms — interactive visualisations of binary search, merge sort, and quick sort with step-by-step breakdowns.
  • Alternative: VisuAlgo — Sorting — animated sorting visualiser where you can step through each algorithm at your own pace.

Linked Lists

Roadmap position: Step 4

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Linked List — covers singly, doubly, and circular lists with code in C++, Java, and Python.
  • Alternative: Apna College — Linked Lists (YouTube) — Hindi-medium walkthrough, popular with Indian B.Tech students.

Stacks & Queues

Roadmap position: Step 5

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Stack Data Structure — clear explanations of push/pop, applications, and implementation choices.
  • Alternative: Abdul Bari — Stack and Queue (YouTube) — concise, board-style teaching with excellent diagrams.

Hashing

Roadmap position: Step 6

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Hashing Data Structure — covers hash functions, collision resolution, and common applications.
  • Alternative: HackerRank — Hash Tables (YouTube) — short, animated explanation under 10 minutes.

Recursion

Roadmap position: Step 7

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Introduction to Recursion — starts with factorial and Fibonacci, builds to subsets and backtracking.
  • Alternative: Aditya Verma — Recursion Playlist (YouTube) — beloved for the "IBH" (input-base-hypothesis) method.

Trees

Roadmap position: Step 8

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Binary Tree Data Structure — covers traversals, BST operations, height, depth, and patterns.
  • Alternative: take U forward — Binary Trees Playlist (YouTube) — structured playlist from basics to advanced problems.

Graphs

Roadmap position: Step 9

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Graph Data Structure — covers representations, BFS, DFS, shortest path, and cycle detection.
  • Alternative: William Fiset — Graph Theory Playlist (YouTube) — deep, animated explanations, excellent for visual learners.

Introduction to Dynamic Programming

Roadmap position: Step 10

  • Primary: GeeksforGeeks — Dynamic Programming — starts with overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure, walks through classic problems.
  • Alternative: Aditya Verma — DP Playlist (YouTube) — the most popular DP playlist among Indian engineering students. Teaches the recursion-to-DP conversion pattern.

General-Purpose Resources (Not Topic-Specific)

These platforms cover multiple topics and are useful as ongoing references:

  • GeeksforGeeks — the largest written DSA reference in India. Good for reading about specific algorithms and seeing code in multiple languages.
  • Khan Academy — Algorithms — excellent for visual, interactive learning. Best for foundations.
  • CP Algorithms (e-maxx) — concise algorithm descriptions with code. More advanced; useful once you've finished the basics.
  • take U forward (YouTube) — Raj (Striver)'s channel with structured playlists covering DSA from scratch.

Our recommendation: Pick one resource per topic, go through it fully, and solve the starter problems. Don't hop between 5 different tutorials for the same topic — depth beats breadth when learning.

What We Intentionally Excluded

  • Paid courses: We only list free resources. Many paid courses are good, but our audience is college students.
  • Full university courses (MIT OCW, Stanford, etc.): These are excellent but move at a pace and depth that doesn't match our "starter" scope.
  • Books: Physical/digital books like CLRS are great for depth but not beginner-friendly for self-study. If you want one, try Grokking Algorithms by Aditya Bhargava (not free, but affordable and very visual).
