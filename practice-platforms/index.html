Best Websites to Practice DSA Problems — An Honest Comparison

Choosing a practice platform is one of the first decisions you'll make. The honest answer: any of these will work if you're consistent. But each has strengths that suit different learning styles. Here's our comparison based on what matters to an Indian B.Tech beginner.

Our recommendation: Start with LeetCode for structured problem practice and use GeeksforGeeks for reading explanations. Don't use more than two platforms at once — consistency on one beats dabbling on five.

Side-by-Side Comparison

LeetCode

leetcode.com

Why We Recommend It for Beginners

LeetCode is the de-facto standard for interview preparation worldwide. Most of the starter problems in our Practice Problems page link to LeetCode because:

  • Problems are tagged by topic and difficulty — you can filter by "Arrays" + "Easy" to find exactly what you need.
  • The discussion section has solutions in multiple languages with explanations.
  • Companies' actual interview questions are often from LeetCode's pool.

Drawbacks

  • The premium tier locks some problems and features (company-tagged questions, editorial solutions). The free tier is more than enough for beginners.
  • Problem descriptions can be terse — if you don't understand one, look it up on GeeksforGeeks for a clearer explanation.
  • It can feel overwhelming with 2,800+ problems. Stick to our curated starter set instead of browsing randomly.

GeeksforGeeks (GFG)

geeksforgeeks.org

Why It's Great for Reading

GFG is the largest free DSA reference site, especially popular in India. Its strengths for beginners:

  • Detailed written explanations with code in C++, Java, and Python.
  • Topic-wise article organisation — great as a reference when you're stuck on a concept.
  • DSA Self-Paced Course (free tier) provides a structured path.

Drawbacks

  • Ad-heavy UI can be distracting. Consider using an ad blocker for a better reading experience.
  • The coding judge is less polished than LeetCode's — test case feedback is sometimes unclear.
  • Quality varies across articles; some are better than others.

HackerRank

hackerrank.com

Best For: Complete Beginners and Language Learning

  • Structured learning tracks (e.g., "30 Days of Code") that guide you step by step.
  • Very clean interface with good test case feedback.
  • Skill certifications that some companies use in their hiring process.

Drawbacks

  • Problems skew easier — once you've done the starter set, you'll outgrow it quickly.
  • Less used for interview prep in India compared to LeetCode.

Codeforces

codeforces.com

Best For: Competitive Programming Enthusiasts

  • Regular contests (Div 1, 2, 3, 4) with a strong community.
  • Editorials and community discussions for every problem.
  • Rating system motivates consistent practice.

Drawbacks

  • Not beginner-friendly. Problems are not topic-tagged well, and the difficulty ramp is steep.
  • UI is functional but dated.
  • More useful for competitive programming than for interview preparation.

CodeChef

codechef.com

Best For: Monthly Contests and the Indian CP Community

  • Strong Indian user base and community.
  • Monthly long challenges give you time to solve harder problems.
  • Good beginner-friendly contests (Starters series).

Drawbacks

  • Problem statements are often lengthy and can confuse beginners.
  • Less commonly asked about in placement interviews compared to LeetCode.

Our Pick: A Two-Platform Strategy

If you're following our DSA Roadmap, here's what we suggest:

  1. Learn the concept from the resource we link (usually GeeksforGeeks or Khan Academy).
  2. Solve the starter problems on LeetCode using our Practice Problems list.
  3. If you want contests, try CodeChef Starters (beginner-friendly) or Codeforces Div 3/4.

Key principle: Platform-hopping is a form of procrastination. Pick one, stick with it for at least a month, and focus on solving problems consistently.
